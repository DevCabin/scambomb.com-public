Is This a Scam?

Quick-Reference Checklist

1

The 10-Second Scam Check

#Ask yourselfRed flag
1Did I expect this message?Surprise delivery, bank alert, job offer, or refund.
2Does it create urgency or fear?“Act now,” suspension threats, penalties, or benefits expiring.
3Does it ask me to click or call?Links or phone numbers supplied inside the message.
4Is the sender slightly off?Lookalike domain, random number, or misspelled company name.
5Does it ask for money or personal information?Password, PIN, SSN, gift cards, crypto, wire transfer, or remote access.
The one rule that protects you: If you’re unsure, contact the company using a phone number or website you already know. Never use contact information in a suspicious message.
!

What Real Companies Never Do

  • Ask for your password, PIN, or full Social Security number by text.
  • Demand payment by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency.
  • Ask you to move money to a “safe” account.
  • Require remote access to your computer.
  • Pressure you to act before you have time to verify.
2

Spot the Link Trap

Hover over links on a computer without clicking. On a phone, don’t tap at all. Watch for shortened URLs, extra characters, and lookalike domains such as USPS-delivery.net instead of USPS.com.

3

The Top 5 Text Scams Right Now

Fake Package Delivery

There’s a problem with your delivery and a link to “fix” it. USPS does not send unsolicited texts with links or charge redelivery fees.

Phony Job Offers

Easy money, no experience needed, reply now. The “job” eventually asks for an equipment fee, training deposit, or money transfer.

Fake Fraud Alerts

“Did you make this purchase?” Replying confirms your number is active. Real banks never ask you to move money to a safe account.

Bogus Unpaid Tolls

A small balance and urgent payment link lead to a fake page that steals your card number. Log in to your toll account directly.

“Wrong Number” Texts

A friendly stranger keeps chatting and eventually pitches an investment. No stranger who texts you by mistake needs to become your friend. Block and delete.

4

If You Think It’s a Scam

  1. Stop. Don’t click, call, or reply.
  2. Screenshot it. Save evidence for reporting.
  3. Verify independently. Type the company’s URL manually or find its real number yourself.
  4. Delete and block.
  5. Report it. Forward texts to 7726 (SPAM) and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  6. Warn your family. If you got it, they might too.
Remember: Real companies give you time. Scammers steal it.
5

How to Report a Scam Text

  • Forward the message to 7726 (SPAM).
  • Use “Report Junk” in your messaging app.
  • File a report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  • For USPS-related scams, email spam@uspis.gov.

B

If You Already Clicked

  • Disconnect from the internet if you downloaded anything.
  • Change passwords immediately—start with email and banking.
  • Contact your bank if you entered financial information.
  • Freeze your credit at all three bureaus. It’s free.
  • Run a malware scan on your device.
  • File a report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and IdentityTheft.gov.
